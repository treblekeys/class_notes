# Final Review Flashcards

## 5G Networks
- The fifth generation of mobile network technology, characterized by
  substantially higher data transmission speeds, lower latency, and greater
  capacity that 4G LTE networks. The textbook examines 5G in terms of its
  enabling role for Internet of Things, autonomous vehicles, remote surgery,
  and other latency sensitive applications

## Agile Manifesto
- A 2001 declaration by seventeen software practitioners articulating four
  values and twelve principles that prioritize individuals and interations,
  working software, customer collaboration, and responsiveness to change over
  rigid planes and documentation

## Agile Methodologies
- A family of iterative software development approaches (including Scrum,
  Extreme Programming, and Kanban) emphasizing short delivery cycles,
  cross-functional teams, continuous feedback, and incremental refinement of
  requirements

## Allocation (Information Systems funding)
- A method of funding information systems where the cost of services is billed
  to the organizational function that uses them based on some stable metric
  (e.g., size, revenues, number of users).

## Artificial Intelligence
- The simulation of human cognitive functions by computer systems, including
  learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, and natural language
  understanding. In the textbook, AI is treated primarily as a managerial
  resource rather than a technical discipline, with emphasis on its
  implications for decision-making, automation, and competitve strategy. 

## Assembly Line
- A production arrangement in which a partially assembled product moves
  sequentially past stationary workers, each performing a specialized task;
  introduced at scale by ford in 1913 and foundational to mass production. 

## Augemented Reality
- A technology that superimposes digital information, images, or objects onto a
  live view of the physical environment, typically through a smartphone,
  tablet, or headset. Distinguished from virtual reality in that the user
  remains anchored to the physical world. 

## Autonomy (smart device capability)
- The capacity of a smart device to execute tasks, adapt to environmental
  conditions, and make decisions without continuous human intervention; one of
  the defining capabilities of smart, connected products.

## Backdoors
- Code built into software programs to allow access to an application by
  circumventing password protection

## Be Able to Prove It (privacy guideline)
- The privacy management principle that an organizaiton must maintain
  documented records, audit trails, and verifiable evidence demonstrating
  compliance with its stated privacy commitments.

## Bitcoin
- The first and most widely adopted cryptocurrency, launched in 2009 and built
  on blockchain technolog, enabling peer-to-peer digital value transfer without
  a central monetary authority.

## Blockchain
- A distributed, append-only digital ledger in which records are grouped into
  cryptographically linked blocks and replicated across a peer-to-peer network,
  making retroactive alteration computationally infeasible without consensus
  from the network. The textbook examines blockchain primarily in the context
  of supple chain transparency, financial settlement, and smart contracts.

## Board of Directors Risk Categories
- The classification of enterprise risk into categories that the board of
  directors is responsible for overseeing, typically including strategic,
  operation, financial, compliance, and reputational risk.

## Botnet
- A network of comprised internet-connect devices, infected with malware and
  remotely controlled by an attacker, commonly used to launch coordinated
  distributed denial-of-service attacks, send spam, or distributed malware.

## Brute Force Password Attack
- An attack in which an adversary systematically tries every possible character
  combination until the correct password is found; defenses include long
  passwords, account lockouts, and rate limiting.

## Build Phase (SDLC)
- The build phase of the SDLC is concerned with taking the system requirements
  document and producing a robust, secure, and efficient software application.

## Business Continuity Risk
- The risk that disruptive events (natural disasters, cyberattacks, supplier
  failures) will interrupt the organization's ability to deliver products or
  services; mitigated through continuity planning and recovery procedures.

## Business Sotware Alliance (BSA)
- An industry trade group representing major software publishers that conducts
  anto-piracy enforcement, advocates for intellectual property protection, and
  audits organizations for software license compliance.

## Careless Behavior
- The category of internal security theat arising from employee negligence
  rather than malicious intent, including weak passwords, unattended devices,
  lost equipment, and inadvertent disclosure of sensitive data.

## Chargeback (IS funding)
- A method of funding information systems where the cost of services is billed
  to the organizational function that uses them based on actual usage.

## Choice (fair information practice)
- The fair information practice principle that individuals must be given
  meaningful options regarding how their personal data is collected, used, and
  shared, including opt-in or opt-out mechanisms.

## Closed-source (proprietary) Software
- Software whose source code is not made available to licensees; users receive
  only the executable, and modification, redistribution, or inspection of the
  underlying code is prohibited by the license.

## Cloud Computing
- The on-demand delivery of computing resources, including servers, storage,
  databases, networking software, and analytics, over the Internet on a
  pay-as-you-go basis. The textbook distinguishes among infrastructure as a
  service (IaaS), platform as a service (PaaS), and software as a service
  (SaaS), and examines governace, cost, and vendor lock-in as managerial...

## Cobot (collaborative robot)
- A robot designed to work safely alongside human operators in a shared
  workspace, typically equipped with force-limiting sensors and compliance
  mechanisms that allow direct physical interaction without protective cages.

## Code of Conduct (information systems ethics)
- A formal organizational document articulating expected ethical behaviour in
  the use of information systems, covering topics such as data handling,
  acceptable use, intellectual property, privacy, and conflicts of interest.

## Computer-Aided Design (CAD)
- Software used to create, modify, analyze, and document precise two and three
  dimensional engineering designs; foundational to modern product engineering
  and a precursor to digital manufacturing workflows. 

## Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM)
- Software that translate engineering designs (typically from CAD systems) into
  the toolpaths and machine instruction used to control automated production
  equipment such as CNC machines and robotic cells.

## Craft Production
- A pre-industrial mode of manufacturing in which highly skilled workers
  produce one-of-a-kind goods using general-purpose tools, contrasted with the
  standardization and division of labor characteristic of mass production.

## Custom Software Development
- The process by which an organization, or a contracted software house, creates
  a tailored software spplication to address the organization's specific
  information processing needs.

## Cyberterrorism
- Politically or idealogically motivated attacks on information systems,
  networks, or critical infrastructue intended to cause significant disruption,
  fear, or physical harm in pursuit of a broader cause.

## Daily Scrum
- A short, time-boxed meeting (typically 15 minutes) held each day during a
  print in which Scrum team members synchronize work, identify obstacles, and
  plan the twenty-four hours of activity.

## Data Harvesting
- The systematic, often automated collection of personal or behavioral data
  from websites, applications, and connected devices, frequently performed
  without explicit user awareness for purpoese such as profiling or resale.

## Deep Learning
- A machine learning technique based on artificial neural networks that can
  discriminate essential features of an input on the basis of provided data; a
  technique that allows classification of inputs based on inferred features.
  Deep learning underlies many mordern AI applications, including image
  recognition, natural language processing, and recomendation systems.

## Definition Phase (SDLC)
- The phase of the SDLC concerned with clearly idenifying the features of the
  proposed information system.

## Denial-of-service (DoS) attack
- A digital assault carried out over a computer network with the objective to
  overwhelm an online service so as to force it offline.

## DevOps
- A set of practices and cultural norms that integrate software development and
  IT operations to shorten the systems development life cycle and deliver
  features, fixes, and updates more frequently and reliably. Treated as a trend
  in Edition 4 before becoming a standard Chapter 11 topic in Edition 5.

## Dictionary Password Attack
- An attack in which an adversary attempts authentication used a precompiled
  list of common words, phrases, previously leaked passwords; defenses include
  password complexity rules and breach-corpus screening.

## Digital Data Stream (DDs)
- The continuous digital encoding and transmission of data describing a related
  classof events. The flow of these digital representations may be human
  generated (e.g., a tweet) or machine generated (e.g., GPS location)

## Digital Manufacturing
- Manufacturing processes that rely on digital design files and
  computer-controlled fabrication, including 3D printing and computer numerical
  control machining. Digital manufacturing is an additive process, working the
  opposite of traditional subtractive manufacturing techniques.

## Direct Migration
- A system migration approach in which the legacy system is shutdown and the
  new system is brought online at a single cutover point; lowest cost but
  highest risk if the new system fails.

## Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attack
- A cyberattack in which a large number of compromised systems, typically
  organized as a botnet under the attacker's control, simultaneously direct a
  flood of traffic at a target service, rendering it unavailable to legitimate
  users.

## Distributed Ledger
- A database tha tis shared, replicated, and synchronized across multiple nodes
  or geographic locations without a central administrator. Each participant in
  the network holds an identical copy of the record, and changes require
  validation through a consensus mechanism before being appended.

## Do What You Say (privacy guideline)
- The privacy management principle that an organization must operationalize its
  stated privacy policies through actual technical controls, business
  processes, and employee practices.

## Economic feasibility
- The dimension of feasibility analysis accessing whether a proposed
  information system's expected benefits justify its cost over the relevant
  planning horizon, typically through cost-benefit analysis or return on
  investment.

## Encryption
- A technique designed to scramble data so as to ensure that if the wrong
  individuals gain access to the data, they will be unable to make out its
  meaning.

## End-User Development
- The process by which an organization's non-IT specialists create software
  applications.

## Exploit
- A specific piece of code, technique, or sequence of commands that takes
  advantage of a vulnerability in software, firmware, hardware to cause
  unintended behavior such as privilege escalation, data exfiltration, or
  denial of service.

## External Intrusion Threat
- The intrusion threat consists of any situation where an unauthorized attacker
  gains access to organizational IT resources; the external variant orginates
  outside the organization's network perimeter.

## Fair Information Practices
- A set of internationally recognized principles for the ethical handling of
  personal data, typically including notice, choice, access, integrity, and
  enforcement; foundational to most modern privacy regulations.

## Feasibility Analysis
- The structured evaluation of a proposed information systems project across
  multiple dimensions (technical, economic, operational, and sometimes legal
  and schdule) to determine whether to proceed with development.

## Firewall
- A network security device of software application that monitors and filters
  network traffice between network segments - typically between a trusted
  internal network and an untrusted external network - based on a defined set
  of securty rules. Firewalls inspect packet headers, connection states, or
  application-layer content to permit or block traffic.

## Function Creep
- The gradual expansion of the scope of a system, dataset, or surveillance
  capability beyond the purposes for which it was orginally collected or
  authorized; a recurring concern in privacy and ethics.

## General Motors (Unimate deployment)
- The 1961 deployment of the Unimate rebot at the General Motors plant in
  Trenton, New Jersey, marking the first industrial use of a programmable
  robotic arm and the beginning of modern factory automation.

## Gold Standard
- A reference dataset of inputs labeled with known-correct outputs by human
  experts, used to train supervised learning models and to evaluate the
  accuracy of model predictions.

## Hacktivism
- The use of hacking techniques to advance political or social causes,
  typically through website defacement, data leaks, or denial-of-service
  attacks; distinguished from financially motivated cybercrime by ideological
  aims.

## IBM Deep Blue
- The IB< chess-playing computer that, in 1997, defeated reigning world
  champion Garry Kasparov in a six-game match; a landmark demonstration of
  specialized AI's capacity to surpass human performance in a constrained
  domain.

## Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover (NIST core functions)
- The five core functions of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, which togehter
  provide a structured taxonomy for organizing an organization's cybersecurity
  activities and outcomes across the full incident lifecyle.

## Implementation Phase (SDLC)
- The phase of the SDLC concerned with taking the technology component and
  integrating it with the other elements (people, process, structures) to
  achieve a working information system.

## Individual Project Risk
- The risk associated with a single information systems project, including the
  likelihood of cost overruns, schedule slippage, scope changes, and outright
  failure to deliver expected functionality. 

## Industry 4.0
- The fourth industrial industrial revolution, characterized by the integration
  of cyber-physical systems, the Industrial Internet of Things, cloud
  computing, big data analytics, AI, and advanced robotics into manufacturing
  processes and supply chains.

## Information Extortion
- A cyberattack in which an adversary threatens to disclose, destroy, or
  withhold access to sensitive data unless a ransom is paid; ransomware is the
  most prevalent contemporary form.

## Information Privacy
- In the context of information systems, the ability of individuals to control
  the terms and conditions under which their personal information is collected,
  managed, and utilized.

## Information Risk
- The exposure to loss arising from threats to the confidentiality, integrity,
  or availability of organizational data assets; a primary concern of
  information security and digital risk management programs.

## Information Security
- The set of defenses an organization puts in place to mitigate threats to its
  technology infrastructure and data resources.

## Information systems Assessment
- The process of taking stock of the firm's current information systems
  resources and evaluating how well they are fulfilling the needs of the
  organization.

## Information Systems Ethics
- the branch of applied ehtics concerned with the moral implications of
  designing, deploying, and using information systems, including quesitons of
  privacy, accuracy, property, and access.

## Information Systems Planning Process
- The process by which the planning team develops the planning documents.

## Information Systems Vision
- A concise statement that captures what the planning team believes should be
  the role of information systems resources in the firm. It provides an
  articulation of the ideal state the firm should strive for in its use and
  management of IS resources.

## Infrastructure Risk
- The risk arising from the underlying technology platform on which information
  systems depend, including hardware failure, network outrages, capacity
  constraints, vendor lock-in, and obsolescence. 

## Intentional Malicious Behavior
- The category of internal security threat in which an authorized insider
  deliberately misuses access privileges to harm the organization, including
  data theft, sabotage, fraud, and unauthorized disclosure.

## Internal Threat
- A securiity threat originating from individuals with legitimate
  organizational access (employees, contractors, business partners),
  encompassing both intentional malicious behavior and unintentional carless
  behavior. 

## Internet of Things (IoT)
- The network of physical objects, vehicles, appliances, and other devices
  embedded with sensors, software, and network connectivity that enables them
  to collect and exchange data. The textbook focuses on the managerial
  implications of continuous data streams from connected devices, including
  service models and operational visibility.

## IP Spoofing
- An attack technique in which the adversary forges the source IP address of
  network packets to impersonate a trusted host, evade access controls, or
  obscure the true origin of an attack.

## ISO 27001
- An international standard published by the International Organization for
  Standardization specifying the requirements for establishing, implementing,
  maintaining, and continually improving an information security management
  system.

## IT Competence Risk
- The risk that an organization lacks the internal skills, expertise, or
  experience needed to specify, evaluate, deploy, or operate information
  systems effectively, leaving it dependent on external providers or unable to
  capture intended value.

## IT Project Risk
- The risk that an information systems development or implementation project
  will fail to deliver the intended functinoality, exceed its budget, or miss
  its deadline, with consequences ranging from sunk costs to operational
  disruption.

## Low-Code / No-Code (LCNC) Development
- Software development platforms and toods that enable the creation of
  functional application through visual interfaces, drag-and-drop components,
  and declarative configuration, with minimal or no hand-written programming.
  Low-code platforms require some technical knowledge; no-code platforms are
  designed for use by business professionals with not programming background.

## Machine Learning
- A subset of artificial intelligence in which systems learn from data to
  improve their performance on a task without being explicitly programmed for
  each specific case. The textbook treats machine learning as a general-purpose
  technology whose managerial importance lies in its applications to
  prediction, classification, and personalzation across industries.

## Malicious Code
- The general term malicious code, or malware, refers to software programs that
  are designed to cause damage to individuals' and/or organizations' digital
  assets.

## Malware
- Short for "malicious software", malware is any program, script, or code
  intentionally designed to damage, disrupt, gain unauthorized access to, or
  exfiltrate data from a computer system, network, or device without the
  consent of the owner. The malware category encompasses viruses
  (self-replicating code that attaches to ligitimate programs), worms, etc

## Man-in-the-middle Attack
- An attack in which the adversary covertly relays and possibly alters
  communications between two parties woh believe they are communicating
  directly with each other; typical defenses include strong encryption and
  certificate validation.

## Mass Production
- A method of producing large quantities of standardized products through
  specialized division of labor, interchangeable parts, and mechanized
  assembly; pioneered in automobile manufacturing in the early twentieth
  century.

## Migraiton Approach
- The chosen strategy for transitioning from a legacy inormation system to a
  new one, with common variants including direct, parallel, phased, and pilot
  migration; selected based on risk tolerance and operational constraints.

## Negative Deliverable (cybersecurity)
- An outcome of a security investment in which success is defined as the
  absence of a harmful event, making return on investment inherently difficult
  to quantify and security spending vulnerable to budget pressure.

## Neural Network
- A computational model loosely inspired by biological neurons, organized in
  interconnected layers tha transform inputs through weighted connections; the
  foundational architecture underlying deep learning.

## NIST Cybersecurity Framework
- A voluntary framework published by the US National Institute of Standards and
  Technology, organized around five core functions (Identify, Protect, Detect,
  Respond, Recover), used by organizations worldwide to structure and benchmark
  cybersecurity programs.

## Notice (fair information practice)
- The fair information practice principle that organizations must provide
  clear, conspicuous, and accessible disclosure of their data collection and
  use practices before personal information is collected.

## Object Code
- The machine-readable output produced by a compiler from human-readable source
  code, consisting of platform-specific instructions executable by a digital
  computer's processor.

## Off-the-shelf Software
- A software program that is mass-produced and commercialized by a software
  vendor.

## Offshoring
- The relocation of business processes (including information systems
  development and operation) to a foreign country, typically motivated by labor
  cost differences, talent availability, or time-zone coverage.

## Open Source Software
- A type of software licensing agreement that enables the licensee to obtain
  and modify the source code of a software program.

## Operational Feasibility
- The dimension of feasibility analysis assessing whether the proposed
  information system can be effectively integrated into existing business
  processes and accepted by the people who will use it.

## Outsourcing
- The process of contracting with an outside firm to obtain information systems
  services.

## Overhead (Information System funding)
- A method of funding information systems where the cost of services is not
  billed to the organizational function that uses them. Rather, IS assets and
  services are funded directly from the organization's overall budget.

## Packet Sniffer
- A tool, hardware or software, that captures and inspects network traffic;
  used legitimately for diagnostics and performance analysis, and
  illegitimately for intercepting credentials, sessions, and other sensitive
  data on unprotected networks.

## Parallel Migration
- A system migration approach in which the legacy and new systems operate
  simultaneously for a defined period, allowing comparison and verification
  before the legacy system is retired; lowest risk but highest cost.

## Pharming
- An attack that redirects users from a legitimate website to a fraudulent one
  without their awareness, typically by manipulating DNS resoution or
  compromising the user's local network configuration.

## Phased Migration
- A system migration approach in which the new system replaces the legacy
  system one funcitonal area or module at a time over an extended period,
  balancing risk against the complexity of running parallel partial systems.

## Phishing
- A social engineering attack in which an adversary impersonates a trusted
  entity, such as a financial institution, enterprise application, government
  agency, or senior executive, through deceptive email, text message, or
  fraudulent website, with the intent of deceiving the target into disclosing
  credentials, financial information, or other sensitive data, or into
  installing malware...

## Pilor Migration
- A system migration approach in which the new system is fully deployed at one
  organizational unit or location first, with broader rollout following only
  after successful operation at the pilot site.

## Polymorphic Threat
- Malicious code that automatically alters its observable form (file signature,
  instruction sequences, encryption key) on each propagation in order to evade
  signature-based detection by antivirus tools.

## Product Owner (Scrom Role)
- The Scrum role responsible for maximizing the value of the product, owning
  and prioritizing the product backlog, and serving as the single authoritative
  voie on product requirements to the development team.

## Programmable GPU
- A graphics processing unit whose parallel processing pipeline can be
  controlled by user-written programs (shaders, compute kernels), enabling its
  use for general-purpose computation including the training of deep learning
  models.

## Programming (SDLC sub-process)
- The sub-process within the SDLC build phase in which programmers translate
  the system design specifications into working source code in one or more
  programming languages.

## Proprietary Software
- Software distributed under a license that restricts the licensee's rights to
  inspect, modify, or redistribute the underlying course code; the dominant
  commercial software model and a contrast to open-source licensing.

## Quantum Computing
- A computing paradigm that uses the properties of matter at the quantum level,
  superposition and entanglement, to perform certain calculations exponentially
  faster than classical computers. The textbook introduces quantum computing in
  Edition 5 as an emergin technology with implications for cryptography, drug
  discovery, financial modeling, and optimization problems, while noting that
  commercially...

## Quantum Entanglement
- A quantum mechanical phenomenon in which two or more particles become linked
  such that the quantum state of each cannot be described independently of the
  other, even when separated by large distances; a foundational resource for
  certain quantum computing algorithms.

## Qubit
- The basic unit of quantum information, analogous to the classical bit but
  capable of existing in a superposition of zero and one states simultaneously,
  enabling quantum computers to evaluate many possibilities in parallel. 

## Ransomware
- A malware that limits the access to a computer system or users' data and that
  requires the user to pay a ransom to regain control.

## Request for Proposal (RFP)
- A formal procurement document issued by an organization seeking competitive
  bids from prospective vendors to supply a specified information system,
  service, or solution; structures vendor responses for comparable evaluation.

## Risk Acceptance
- A risk response strategy in which the organization formally acknowledges a
  risk and elects to bear its consequences without taking specific mitigating
  action, typically because the cost of mitigation exceeds the expected loss. 

## Risk Analysis
- The process by which the firm attempts to quantify the risks identified in
  the risk assessment.

## Risk Assessment
- The risk assessment process consists of auditing the current resources,
  technological as well as human, in an effort to map the current state of the
  art of information systems security in the organization.

## Risk Audit
- A periodic, structured review of an organizaiton's risk identification,
  assessment, and mitigation activities to verify that risk management
  processes are operating as designed and producing reliable outputs.


## Risk Mitigation
- The process of matching the appropriate response to the security threats a
  firm has identified. 

## Risk Reduction
- A risk response strategy in which controls and countermeasures are introduced
  to lower either the likelihood or the impact of an identified risk to within
  the organization's risk tolerance. 

## Risk Transference
- A risk response strategy in which the financial consequences of a risk are
  shifted to a third party, typically through insurance, contractual
  indemnification, or outsourcing arrangements.

## Say What You Do (privacy guideline)
- The privacy management principle that an organization must publicly disclose
  its data collection, use, sharing, and retention practices through clear and
  accessible policy documentation.

## Scrum
- The most-used agile software development methodology that progresses via a
  series of iterations called sprints, lasting usually two to four weeks.

## Scrum Master
- The Scrum role responsible for facilitating the Scrum process, removing
  impediments to the team's progress, coaching team members on Scrum practices,
  and shielding the team rom external interference.

## Serverless Computing
- A cloud execution model in which the provider dynamically allocates compute
  resources to run discrete functions on demand, biling customers only for
  actual execution time and freeing developers from server provisioning and
  capacity planning.

## Smart Device
- An electronic device that combines embedded sensors, processing capability,
  and network connectivity to collect data, exchange information, and respond
  to environmental conditions; a constituent component of IoT systems.

## Social Engineering
- The practice of obtaining restricted or private information by somehow
  convincing legitimate users or people who have it to share it.

## Software & Information Industry Association (SIIA)
- An industry trade association representing software and digital content
  publishers that conducts anti-piracy enforcement, public policy advocacy, and
  industry research on behalf of its member companies.

## Software Piracy
- The unauthorized copying, distribution, installation, or use of copyrighted
  software in violation of the terms of its license; a sustained focus of
  industry enforcement by groups such as the BSA and SIIA.

## Software Selection Process
- The process by which an organization identifies and purchases an
  off-the-shelf software application to address its information processing
  needs.

## SolarWinds Breach
- The 2020 supply chain cyberattack in which adversaries inserted malicious
  code into the SolarWinds Orion network management software update process,
  compromising thousands of customer organizations including multiple US
  federal agencies.

## Source Code
- Human-readable instructions written by a programmer in a programming
  language, which a compiler or interpreter then translates into the executable
  form (object code or bytecode) that a computer can run.

## Spoofing
- The general category of attack in which an adversary disguises a
  communication, identity, or system as originating from a trusted source,
  encompassing variants such as IP spoofing, email spoofing, caller ID
  spoofing, and website spoofing.

## Sprint
- A fixed-length iteration in the Scrum methodology, typically two to four
  weeks, during which a Scrum team commits to delivering a defined increment of
  working software.

## Spyware
- Software that, unbeknownst to the owner of the computer, monitors behavior,
  collects information, either transfers this information to a third party via
  the Internet or performs unwanted operations.

## Steering Committee
- A committee, composed of representatives from the various functional areas
  and IS professionals, that provides guidance for the use of information
  systems assets and shared the responsibility for aligning IS efforts with
  business strategy.

## Strategic Business Planning
- An organization's mission and future direction, performance targets, and
  strategy. Strategic plans are a prerequisite to information systems planning.

## Strategic Initiatives
- A long-term (three to five year) proposal that identifies new systems and new
  projects or new directions for the IS organization.

## Superposition
- A quantum mechanical principle by which a quantum system can exist in a
  combination of multiple basis states simultaneously until measured; the
  property that gives qubits their parallel computational potential.

## Supervised Machine Learning
- A machine learning paradigm in which a model is trained on a labeled dataset
  of input-output pairs, learning a function that maps inputs to the correct
  outputs and can then predict labels for new, unseen inputs.

## Surface Mount Technology (SMT)
- A method of constructing electronic circuits by mounting components directly
  onto the surface of a printed circuit board, enabling smaller, lighter,
  denser, and more easily automated assemblies than the older through-hole
  method.

## System Design
- The activity within the SDLC build phase in which the system architect
  translates the system requirements into a detailed specification of the
  system's structure, components, interfaces, and data flows.

## Systems Development Life Cycle (SDLC)
- A general engineering approach to system design, development, and
  implementation. The SDLC process articulates in three phases: definition,
  build, and implementation, each further divided into three steps.

## Technical Feasibility
- The dimension of feasibility analysis assessing whether the proposed
  information system can be built or acquired with available technology,
  expertise, and time, given the requirements specified for it. 

## Testing (SDLC sub-process)
- The sub-process within the SDLC build phase in which the developed software
  is systemcatically exercised against its requirements to detect defects,
  including unit, integration, system, and acceptance testing.

## Threat
- Any potential cause of an unwanted incident that may result in harm to an
  information system, its data, or the organization that depends on it; threats
  may be intentional or accidental, internal or external.

## Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)
- A financial estimate designed to explicitly recognize the life cycle cost of
  IT assets.

## Trojan Horse
- A computer program that claims to, and sometimes does, deliver some useful
  functionality. But the Trojan horse hides a dark side and, like a virus,
  delivers a malicious payload.

## Unimate Robot
- The first industrial robot, developed by George Devol and Joseph Engelberger
  and deployed at General Motors in 1961, performing die-casting and welding
  tasks; the foundational artifact of modern factory automation.

## Unsupervised Machine Learning
- A machine learning paradigm in which the model is given input data without
  labeled outputs and must discover structure on its own, including techniques
  such as clustering, dimensionality reduction, and association rule mining.

## Virtual Reality
- A technology that immerses users in a fully computer-generated environment,
  replacing their perception of the physical world with a simulated one,
  typically through a head-mounted display. Distinguished from augmented
  reality in that the physical surroundings are occluded entirely.

## Virus
- A type of malicious code that spreads by attaching itself to other
  legitimate, executable software programs.

## Vulnerability
- A weakness in an information system, security procedure, internal control, or
  implementation that could be exploited by a threat to compromise
  confidentiality, integrity, or availability.

## WannaCry
- The 2017 ransomware worm that exploited a Windows SMB vulnerability
  (EternalBlue) to spread rapidly across hundreds of thousands of systems
  worldwide, encrypting victim data and demanding Bitcoin ransom; notably
  disrupted the UK's National Health Service. 

## Whaling
- A targeted phishing variant directed specifically at senior executives, board
  members, or other high-value individuals, typically using carefully
